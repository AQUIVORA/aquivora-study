Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic

Every composite number can be expressed as a product of primes, and this factorisation is unique apart from the order of the prime factors.

Example: 60 = 2² × 3 × 5

Irrational Numbers

An irrational number cannot be written in the form p/q, where p and q are integers and q ≠ 0.

Examples include √2, √3 and π.

Operations on Real Numbers

Irrational numbers do not remain irrational under every operation. For example, √3 × √3 = 3 and √3 + (−√3) = 0.

When proving a number such as √2 is irrational, a contradiction argument can be used.

Key Points

  • Prime factorisation is unique apart from order.
  • Use lowest terms in p/q proofs.
  • Not every operation on irrational numbers gives an irrational result.
